## Deviations from the brief
1. `SuWorldDesc` layout is unchanged. Adding `max_entities` would give out-of-scope callers (`src/runtime/worker.c`, `tests/vm`, `tests/native/*`, `tests/scheduler`) an uninitialized capacity. The capacity is passed through the new `su_world_create_ex(desc, max_entities, out)`; `su_world_create` is unchanged. Owner decision needed if the struct field is still wanted.
2. Spawn and despawn act on the committed root (like `init_committed`); there is no world revision field, so no revision hook was added. The revision string stays with the worker.
3. The world's `entity_ids` array is gone; external ids live in the entity table. Fixture behaviour is identical.
4. `su_world_row_count` returns capacity. Equal to n for fixtures.
5. Snapshot capture always writes 0.2. Descriptor entity IDs are checked only against slots alive at their first generation (gen 1): a reused slot legitimately differs from the descriptor. Parse applies the same rule. The runtime's descriptor after churn therefore must not be assumed to match.
6. In 0.2, `entity_ids` holds high_water rows (external ids of the slots); `columns` hold high_water rows.
7. `su_world_publish` also zeroes dead rows, and `su_snapshot_restore` pads columns to capacity and calls `su_world_adopt_entities`. Two public additions were needed for that: `su_world_adopt_entities` and `su_world_restore_canonical`.
8. Test seams: tests cast `su_world_entities_*` to non-const to call `su_ents_fault_set_generation` and to despawn in a working table inside a tick (abort and publish discipline). Test-only.
