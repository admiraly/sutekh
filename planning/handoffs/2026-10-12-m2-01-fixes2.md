## Design

- N1: `pin` is the rollback target of the last committed migration. A commit stages `pin_try` (the checkpoint before it),
  promotes it on success, and the wrapper clears it on every exit. A failed attempt (prepare refused, swap refused,
  undone post-commit checkpoint) leaves `pin` and `pin_mig` unchanged. This reverses handoff deviation 2 (rollback is
  now available after a failed commit).
- N2: an injection point is one allocation attempt (refused before it is made) or one explicit prepare point.
  `points` counts the points of one hooks struct across a plan and the prepare request that replans.
- Native records: the commit clears the supervisor's `nat[]` records (the swap released the old schedule's kernels,
  worker `su_sched_free`); rollback clears them too. An undo does not clear them: the undone world is rebuilt like after
  a worker death, and the records are kept as they were (same policy as deaths).

## Decisions (owner)

- **N4 (recovery exhaustion inside an undo), documented, not unified.** Three worker deaths during the undo's rebuild:
  if the death is in a rebuild request, the world is marked unrecoverable (`SU_E_INTERNAL`); if it is in `seed_worker`,
  the reply is `SU_E_RESOURCE_LIMIT` with the pre-state and the next request rebuilds. Both fail closed. This is the
  general M1 `recover` policy; unifying it changes M1 behaviour and is not a migration change. Owner to choose.
- **N5 (IDs of an undone commit), left as is.** A commit undone by the post-commit undo leaves its field IDs unretired:
  a fresh plan may add ID 3 again. This matches "an unacknowledged attempt never happened". Contract B5 read literally
  refuses it. The owner should state the intended reading; until then this is a deviation.
- **Deviation 2 reversed**: after a failed commit the rollback target is still the last committed migration.
