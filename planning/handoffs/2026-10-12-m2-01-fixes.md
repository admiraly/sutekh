## Design of the post-commit fix (BLK-1)

Contract section 1 says a failed migration leaves the old world untouched; section 4 (5) says a commit without a
durable post-commit checkpoint is never acknowledged. The supervisor's log is the recovery source, so the undo removes
the `LOG_MIGRATE` entry and restores the pre-commit revision, tick, digest, schedule manifest and migrated flag. A
replacement worker then rebuilds the pinned pre-migration checkpoint and replays the log that ends before the
migration (each replayed digest is checked). Nothing is logged for the undo; the world revision after a failed commit
equals the revision before it (the control digest is reproduced exactly).

- Double fault: `SU_E_WORKER_FAILED`, `error.detail = {reason: "post_commit_checkpoint_failed", rolled_back_to:
  "pinned_pre_migration_checkpoint"}`, retry allowed (the retry commits to the control digest).
- No room: `SU_E_RESOURCE_LIMIT`, `error.detail = {reason: "post_commit_checkpoint", rolled_back_to: ...}`.
- The replacement worker that rebuilt the state is reported in the recovery object of the reply.

## Deviations and decisions

1. Post-commit failure is an undo, not a `LOG_ROLLBACK`: the world keeps its pre-commit revision (contract section 1
   reads as "old world untouched"). The reply names the rollback (`rolled_back_to`) as the contract's section 4 text
   requires for the no-room case.
2. After an undo, the pin is no longer a rollback target (`rollback_unavailable` until the next commit). The pin
   replaced by the attempt is the pre-commit state, which is not a committed migration.
3. The undo replaces the worker (a recovery object is attached to the reply), even for the no-room case where the
   worker did not die. This keeps "recovery from the log" as the only way the world changes (AGENTS.md: in-process faults
   need recovery in a replacement worker, not a pointer rollback).
4. `ops: []` is accepted: a migration with no operations is a generation bump that forces resubmission of every
   system. Decision recorded here; the contract does not forbid it.
5. The rollback reply's `lineage` is "retained" only when the worker confirms the undone IDs are retired; otherwise
   the rollback fails and the world is marked unrecoverable.
6. Commit reply `timings.plan_ns` is the worker time of the plan the commit uses (measured when the plan was made);
   `bytes` are the plan's estimates.
7. The swap checks are a separate function (`su_migrate_swap_check`); the publication repeats them and cannot then fail.
